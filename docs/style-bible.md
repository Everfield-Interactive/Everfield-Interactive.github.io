# Style bible: Gilt Fracture

Status: approved at Gate 1 on 2 October 2026 (D42 to D50). Kiefy may revisit any part of it.

This document refines Section 5 of the build guide into rules that can be built from. Every image in `references/` was studied for it, and every colour token was checked against the image it names.

## 1. The idea

The world is painted. The interface is porcelain. Where porcelain breaks, the edge is gilded, and the break shows crystal light or the world behind it.

The break is the one loud idea. It is the loading crack, the edge of every plate, the way a jump opens, and the wound in the monolith. Everything else stays quiet.

## 2. How to use the references

- The references show what Kiefy likes. None is an asset or a layout to copy.
- A proposal or review that cites a reference gives its ID and says what it takes.
- The "Leave" column is a list of things that would count as copying.
- No reference image, and no part of one, is ever shipped or committed.

## 4. What the references agree on

These qualities appear across the set, and Section 5 of the guide does not state them. They are proposed as rules.

- Each frame has one brightest point, and it sits on the focal object.
- A dark foreground band frames a lit middle ground, so the scene makes its own vignette.
- Distance comes in clear steps, and each place has its own fog colour.
- Shadows change hue as well as value. Warm light sits against cool shadow.
- Grass runs from a dark base to a light tip.
- A tall form loses one end into haze or cloud.
- Interface grounds are never flat fills. They carry a faint wash or grain.
- Interface gold stays in the middle values and is bright only at a highlight.
- One small saturated accent, about 1% of the frame, is enough to pull the eye.

## 5. Materials

| Material | From | Looks like | Used for | Never |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Porcelain | R18 | A neutral white glaze with soft reflections and visible thickness at the edge | Plates, buttons, the monolith's shell | Flat white rectangles with drop shadows |
| Cobalt | R18, R23 | A crisp blue pattern under the glaze | Filigree on porcelain, links, small ornament | Large fills, or any copied historical pattern |
| Gilt | R18, R17 | Gold leaf: uneven, slightly raised, bright at the edge | The seam of every break, rules, diamonds, dial lines | Body text on porcelain, or a flat yellow gradient |
| Geode | R18, R09 | Broken crystal, teal to deep blue, with glints that move as the view moves | Inside breaks, focus and active states, the monolith core | Large calm areas in the interface, or use without a break round it |
| Ink | R14, R17, R03 | Near-black with a soft wash at its edges | Full-screen grounds, silhouettes, text | Pure black in the interface |
| Paint | R04, R05, R11, R15, R19 | Visible brush planes and canvas grain | The whole 3D world | Photoreal materials or photo textures |

- The cobalt pattern is original. It is drawn from the site's own world: wheat ears, field flowers and orbit lines.
- On a large plate the reflections stay soft and low, so that text on the plate stays readable.
- The guide calls porcelain "warm-white" and ink "warm". The sampled tokens are neutral, and they stand. Warmth comes from the wash and the light, not from the base colour.

### 6.2 Rules

- The painted world carries the colour. The interface is porcelain or ink plus one accent.
- In the interface, geode covers less than about 5% of a frame. The Core is the one place where geode is the setting.
- Crimson belongs to secrets. In the interface it appears only where a secret is hinted or found. In the world it is the colour of the Crystal Wood.
- Violet appears only as flat facets and dust in the Crystal Wood and the Shard Desert. It is never a gradient.
- The night colours are a cosmetic palette that a secret unlocks.

## 7. Type

- The wordmark is custom lettering, drawn in Phase 4 in the direction of R02 and R03. Until then the studio name is set in the display face.
- The site uses two families at most, plus the wordmark. Font files are hosted with the site.
- The chosen pairing is D: Bodoni Moda for display and Jost for text (D43). `lab/type/` shows it in text that already exists.

| Pairing | Display | Text | What it gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Bodoni Moda | EB Garamond | The condensed, high-contrast display of R01 and R02. Serif labels and captions, which no reference shows |
| B | Cormorant | Jost | A softer, bookish display. Sans labels and captions, as in R13 and every caption in the set |
| C | Marcellus | Cormorant Garamond | Classical capitals, as in R10 and R12. Its numerals drop below the line, and its text face is light at body size |
| D | Bodoni Moda | Jost | The display of A with the text of B. It is an addition to the three in the guide |

Scale, for whichever pairing is chosen:

| Role | Size | Line height | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chapter title | `clamp(3.5rem, 9.5vw, 9rem)` | 0.92 | Display face, set tight at -0.02em, with lining numerals |
| Wordmark stand-in | `clamp(2rem, 4.2vw, 3.75rem)` | 1.05 | Display face |
| Second title | 1.75rem | 1.2 | Display face |
| Body | 1.125rem | 1.55 | Text face, at most 65 characters to a line |
| Control and label | 1rem to 1.125rem | 1.4 | Text face |
| Notice | 0.875rem | 1.55 | Text face |
| Caption | 0.8125rem | 1.4 | Text face, tracked at 0.04em, in sentence case |

- A caption appears only when it carries information: the chapter number, the place on the path, the sound state.
- Copy is in sentence case. A control says what it does.
- Avoid one word of a headline picked out in another style, a label in capitals above every heading, and arrows added to links.

## 8. Line-work and ornament

- Orbits are thin circles and arcs, concentric with the thing they annotate.
- Hairlines may run edge to edge and pass through an object, which ties the object to the frame.
- Dials take compass and astrolabe forms, for navigation and progress.
- Moon phases are a row of discs that show chapter progress.
- Diamonds and thin rules separate things.
- Glyph rings are nested rings whose centres shift towards one point, so they have depth. Their marks come from the studio script.
- Ink wash and gold-leaf edges are for full-width grounds.
- All ornament is SVG or shader-drawn.
- All ornament in one view shares one line weight and one colour.
- A view shows at most two kinds of ornament. The fixed frame is counted apart from the plate (Q16).

## 9. Layout

1. Compose each chapter like a poster: one subject in the world, one block of type, and empty space.
2. Decide which third the subject holds, then put the type in the opposite third.
3. Layouts are asymmetric. Centre alignment is kept for the threshold and the wordmark.
4. Text always sits on a ground: a porcelain plate, an ink panel, or a part of the frame the camera keeps calm.
5. Use a 12-column grid with outer margins of `clamp(24px, 5vw, 96px)`.
6. Five items are fixed to the frame on a desktop: the mark, the dial, the ledger, chapter progress and the options control. A phone keeps the mark, the dial and the options control.
7. Use strong scale contrast: one very large title or numeral, small exact labels, and little in between.
8. A plate is a porcelain shape with one broken, gilded edge. It is never one of a grid of rounded boxes.
9. On a phone the world fills the top of the screen, and the plate becomes a sheet at the bottom, at most half the height.

`lab/layout/field.html` is the layout sheet for the Field at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844. Kiefy approves the sheets at Gate 3.

## 10. Motion

- Movement is slow and eased, with no bounce.
- The world is always alive. The interface stays still until the visitor acts.
- Each chapter has one orchestrated moment, when the title arrives as the camera settles.
- The fracture is the only transition device.
- The wind has one direction across the whole world, as the strokes in R04 lean one way.
- With reduced motion, camera travel becomes cross-fades between held views, and idle motion stops.

- The monolith floats, and its lower end fades into haze (R08).
- The colour of each chapter's fog is set in look-dev and stored with the world data.

## 12. Secrets and notes

- A secret is hinted by a faint shimmer with a pale core and a set rhythm. It is recognised by its motion and its pale core, never by colour alone.
- A found secret is sealed in crimson.
- A development note is marked in gilt and is never hidden.
- The ledger is a porcelain plate with 80 slots grouped by chapter, plus a group called Pages.

## 13. The mark

- A monogram of E and I, joined as one sign, with the faceted cut of R14 and the thorned serifs of R02 and R03.
- A ring of golden text in an original script that spells the studio name.
- Gilt on ink is the main version.
- Three options are drawn in Phase 4, for Kiefy to choose from at Gate 4.

## 14. Do not

- The default three.js look: grey standard materials, orbit controls, glow on everything.
- Starfields, particles with no source, purple or neon gradients, frosted glass panels.
- Identical rounded cards, or drop shadows under white boxes.
- Stock or generated imagery, or emoji used as icons.
- Text over busy 3D with no ground.
- Lorem ipsum, invented lore, or generated tones as sound.
- More than one loud idea in a frame.
